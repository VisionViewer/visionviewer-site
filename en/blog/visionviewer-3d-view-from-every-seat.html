VisionViewer: a 3D seat view from every seat in the cinema

VisionViewer gives moviegoers a 3D seat view from the seat they are about to book, in the auditorium they will actually sit in, before they pay. Here is what the product is, who it is for, how it works, and what it does not do.

In short

  • VisionViewer is a tool for cinemas: it shows each moviegoer the view from their seat, in 3D, during online booking and before payment.
  • The view is computed, not photographed. Each auditorium is described by its dimensions; the view is rendered in the browser for the seat being chosen.
  • It plugs into the existing ticketing flow with one script tag and one call. Showtimes, availability and payment stay with the cinema.
  • Two integrations: the 3D view alone, opened from the cinema’s own seat map; or our 2D seat map plus the 3D view, for cinemas that have no seat map.
  • It is built for cinemas, and only for cinemas. Not stadiums, not theaters with balconies.

What is VisionViewer?

VisionViewer is software that shows, inside a cinema’s booking flow, a 3D seat view from the seat the moviegoer is choosing. It does not sell tickets and does not replace the ticketing system: it sits on top of it, at the exact moment the moviegoer picks a seat.

A view from the seat, in a cinema, is the screen as you see it sitting in one specific seat. A seat map tells you where the seat is; the view tells you what you will see from it: how big the screen looks, the angle you watch it from, how far you have to look up, and the rows in front of you.

Who is VisionViewer for?

  • Cinema operators who sell seats online, on their website or in their app, and want moviegoers to pick a seat knowing what they are getting.
  • Cinemas that do not offer an online seat map yet. In France, for example, among cinemas that sell online, 95.7% of chain cinemas let you choose your seat, against 5.1% of cinemas outside chains, according to the CNC, the national film agency. For those cinemas, VisionViewer provides the seat map along with the view.
  • The teams and agencies that build cinema websites: the integration is one tag and one call, and it can be tried right away on a developer’s machine.

It is not for stadiums, concert halls or theaters. Other tools serve them; we describe them in our comparison of view-from-seat software.

How does a view from your seat at the cinema work?

1. The auditorium is described by its dimensions

Each auditorium is rebuilt as it actually is, from its measurements:

  • the screen: its width, its height, the height of its bottom edge, and its curvature if it is curved;
  • the seating rake: the distance from the screen to the first row, the spacing between rows, the height of each step;
  • the seats: their width and the gap between neighbors;
  • the layout: aisles, wheelchair spaces, double seats, and spots with no seat.

No seat map and no measurements? We take them: remotely, from a few photos of the auditorium, or on site, where a VisionViewer engineer measures the screen, the rake, the rows and the spacing.

2. The view is computed for the chosen seat

The camera sits at the eyes of a seated moviegoer, 1.15 m above the floor of their row, and looks at the center of the screen. For a group of seats, it sits at the center of the group: for three seats, the middle one. The cinema’s logo is projected on the screen.

Nothing is photographed, so no seat is left out, and a refitted auditorium is updated by changing its dimensions. The trade-off: the view shows the geometry of the room, not its decor.

3. The view opens inside the booking flow

The moviegoer picks seats, opens the 3D view, and compares. The arrow buttons move the whole group, which keeps its shape and size, to the next available seats. Confirming hands the seats being viewed back to the cinema’s page; going back to the seat map closes the view without changing anything. The moviegoer never leaves the cinema’s site.

Either way, a script tag loads the SDK, then a single call does the rest. The page keeps using its own seat identifiers: if the ticketing system calls G9 “7-9”, the mapping is recorded once, auditorium by auditorium. The code and the steps are in our step-by-step integration guide.

What stays with the cinema?

  • Ticketing. We store no showtimes, no availability, no sales: only the shape of the auditoriums. The cinema’s page passes us the seats already sold, and its ticketing system has the final say at payment.
  • Its moviegoers. No account, no cookie, nothing stored in their browser.
  • Its numbers. VisionViewer counts, per day and per auditorium, seat maps displayed, 3D views opened, confirmed choices and, if the site reports them with reportOrder(), orders. These are counts, with nothing known about the moviegoer.

What VisionViewer does not do

  • It does not sell tickets or take any payment.
  • It does not photograph the auditorium. No lighting, no materials, no decor: the view shows distances, angles and heights.
  • It cannot describe every auditorium yet. The model handles straight rows, on a rake with a constant slope, on a single level. Curved rows and balconies are not supported.
  • It does not take more than nine seats per order, and the seats must be adjacent: side by side, or one in front of the other.
  • It does not promise sales. We have no cinema results to publish yet, and we credit VisionViewer with no gain we have not measured.

How do you get started?

  1. Try the demo, ideally on a phone, where moviegoers will book.
  2. Send us one auditorium: its dimensions, its seat map, or a few photos. We rebuild it.
  3. Your developer tries it locally with a test key (vv_test_…), which works right away on localhost and displays a “test version” notice.
  4. You go live with a live key (vv_live_…), which only works on the domains you declare.

Frequently asked questions

What is a 3D seat view?

It is the screen as a moviegoer sees it from one specific seat. In VisionViewer, it is computed from the auditorium’s dimensions, with the camera 1.15 m above the floor of the row, looking at the center of the screen.

Does a cinema need to change its ticketing system to use VisionViewer?

No. VisionViewer is added to the booking page with one script tag and one call. The ticketing system keeps showtimes, availability and payment; we only store the shape of the auditoriums.

Does VisionViewer work without a seat map?

Yes. With VisionViewer.mountPlan(), our 2D seat map is inserted into the cinema’s page, and the 3D view opens from it. If the cinema has no seat plan at all, we build one from photos, or an engineer measures the auditorium on site.

Which auditoriums can VisionViewer not describe yet?

Auditoriums with curved rows, an irregular rake, or several levels, such as a balcony. The current model describes straight rows, on a rake with a constant slope, on a single level.
