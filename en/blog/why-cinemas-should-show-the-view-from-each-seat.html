Cinema online booking: why show the view from each seat

In cinema online booking, choosing a seat has become a reason to buy, and it increasingly happens on a phone. This piece is for cinema operators and marketing leads: what the studies say, what showing the view from the seat can change, and what it takes.

In short

What does “showing the view from the seat” mean?

It means showing moviegoers, before they pay, the screen as they will see it from the seat they are about to book. The seat map tells them where the seat is; the view tells them what they will see from it: how big the screen looks, the angle, how far they have to look up. It can be a photo, a panorama or a computed 3D view; our comparison of view-from-seat software describes these approaches.

Does seat choice really matter before the purchase?

Yes, according to published studies.

  • In the United States, 28% of online buyers say they chose their cinema because it offers reserved seating, against 7.6% of box-office buyers. The study, commissioned by Webedia Movies Pro from Vertigo Research, surveyed more than 100,000 moviegoers in 2018.
  • In France, 22.4% of moviegoers who book do so in order to choose their seat, and 28.6% of seniors, according to the CNC, the national film agency. It is the third reason given, even though most cinemas outside chains do not offer seat choice online.

For an operator, the takeaway is simple: in a reserved seating cinema, the seat is not a detail of the order. It is one of the reasons to place the order with you. The details are in our piece reserved seating: what the studies say.

Why does the phone change the question?

Because the choice is made on a small screen. In France in 2026, 46.4% of moviegoers who book do it on a smartphone, and 40.9% on a computer. The smartphone’s share has grown by 32.9 points since 2016.

On a phone, a seat map shrinks to a grid of dots. It shows the row and the position in the row; it does not show the size of the screen, the slope of the rake, or the real distance between two rows. A view from the seat shows those proportions, on the same screen, without leaving the page.

It must not slow booking down, though: our own measurements, and their limits, are in a seat map in under a second.

Where do moviegoers hesitate?

When they pick their seat, and for a concrete reason: a row letter means nothing on its own. Row G is in the middle of one auditorium and at the back of another. In the auditorium of our demo, with a screen 9.40 m wide:

Tastes vary too. The French academic Claude Forest observed 3,501 moviegoers in three cinemas: the first to arrive, who have every choice, do not necessarily head for the seats the standards rate best. A general rule is not enough.

And the cinema’s booking flow is where the sale is decided: 72% of moviegoers who find their showtime on the cinema’s website complete their purchase there (Webedia Movies Pro and Vertigo Research, 2018). A doubt about the seat, a tab opened to look for a photo of the auditorium, are so many chances to lose them. This is reasoning, not a result: we know of no study that measures this hesitation in cinemas.

How do you stand out when everyone sells online?

Online sales have become the norm. In France, 68.9% of cinemas sell tickets online in 2025, up from 47.9% in 2018. Seat choice is still uneven: among cinemas that sell online, 95.7% of chain cinemas offer it, against 5.1% outside chains.

Showing the view from the seat is still rare in cinemas. Among the cinema ticketing vendors we reviewed in November 2026, only one, Compeso, advertises a 360° view from the selected seat, in its app. For an independent cinema, it is a way to offer what few competitors offer; for a chain, a way to make the difference between its auditoriums visible. The French figures are in our piece on online booking in French cinemas.

What about premium seats and auditoriums?

A seat sold at a higher price has to show what it is worth. Two facts shed light on this:

  • Moviegoers know where they want to sit. In March 2023, 48% of US adults said they preferred seats in the middle of the theater, and 8% the front third (Morning Consult, 2,203 adults).
  • Price alone does not move people. In 2023, the US chain AMC tested different prices depending on the seat. More than three in four members of its loyalty program who already bought the center seats kept doing so, despite the upcharge; the price cut on the front row attracted almost no additional moviegoers. AMC ended the test.

A wider recliner, a better-placed row, a bigger screen: the view from the seat shows the difference instead of describing it. What this does to premium seat sales has not been measured; we do not claim it.

What these arguments do not prove

  • None of these studies measures the effect of a view from the seat in a cinema.
  • They are mostly self-reported, and the US figures date from 2018 and 2023.
  • We have no cinema results to publish yet. We credit VisionViewer with no gain we have not measured.

Frequently asked questions

Why should a cinema show the view from the seat in online booking?

Because seat choice is a reason to buy online, and that choice is increasingly made on a phone, where a seat map does not show what you will see. The view answers that question before payment, without leaving the page.

Does reserved seating influence which cinema people choose?

According to a 2018 study by Webedia Movies Pro and Vertigo Research, 28% of US online buyers say they chose their cinema because it offers reserved seating, against 7.6% of box-office buyers.

Does a view from the seat increase a cinema’s sales?

We know of no study that measures it in cinemas, and we have no cinema results to publish yet. VisionViewer counts views opened, confirmed choices and reported orders, so that each cinema can measure it against its own numbers.
