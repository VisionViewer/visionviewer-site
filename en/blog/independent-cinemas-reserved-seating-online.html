Independent cinemas and reserved seating online: the gap with the chains

Independent cinema reserved seating is still the exception. France is one of the few countries where it has been measured: among cinemas that sell tickets online, 95.7% of chain cinemas let moviegoers pick their seat, against 5.1% of independents. Here is what the data from France’s national film agency says, what may explain the gap, and how an independent cinema can start without even having a seat map.

In short

In France, choosing your seat online is something the chains offer and the independents mostly do not. Almost every chain cinema that sells online has reserved seating; outside the chains, about one in twenty does. Meanwhile, more and more moviegoers book ahead, especially younger ones, and a share of them book precisely to choose their seat.

The CNC, France’s national film agency, splits cinemas into “chain” and “non-chain” venues. In this article, “independent cinema” means the second group, which includes many arthouse cinemas. The CNC does not publish these figures for arthouse cinemas alone.

How wide is the gap between chains and independents?

The gap widens at every step of the purchase. According to the CNC’s 2025 survey of exhibitors:

StepChainsIndependents
Sell tickets onlineAll53.8%
Of those, let moviegoers choose their seat95.7%5.1%
Of those, sell less than a quarter of tickets online11.4%78.8%
Of those, sell more than half of tickets online33.9%1.0%

The last two rows are the exhibitors’ own estimates. Among single-screen cinemas, online sales drop to 37.6%.

The most striking number is not the first one. Online sales are spreading among independents: more than half offer them. But seat choice has not progressed outside the chains, while it gained 23.6 points among chains since 2021. The CNC sums it up in one phrase: seat choice is the preserve of the chains.

What moviegoers expect

In 2026, 41.7% of French moviegoers booked their seat in advance, up from 25.6% in 2019. Among 25–34-year-olds, 59.1% book; among 15–24-year-olds, 49.7%; among seniors, 28.6%. Almost half of those who book do it on a smartphone: 46.4%.

Why book? First to be sure of getting in (59.7%), then to skip the line (43.9%), and to be able to choose a seat: 22.4%, rising to 28.6% among seniors.

So a moviegoer used to picking a seat at the multiplex finds, at most independent cinemas, a checkout where they pay without knowing where they will sit. Younger audiences, who book the most and do it on their phones, are the ones for whom this matters most. France is the case we can document; we know of no comparable published split between chains and independents for other countries.

Why do so few independent cinemas offer reserved seating?

The CNC measures the gap; it does not explain it. These are the reasons most often heard in the field. They are hypotheses, not data, and most of them apply well beyond France.

  • General admission. Many independent auditoriums have no numbered seats: you walk in and sit wherever you like. It is simple at the box office, and the audience is used to it. The CNC does not say what share of auditoriums have numbered seats.
  • Smaller auditoriums. In a small room at a half-empty screening, picking a seat feels less necessary. It matters again on premiere nights, Q&A screenings and big releases.
  • Tools. Selling numbered seats online requires a ticketing system that supports it, and a numbered, up-to-date seat map of each auditorium. Many independents have neither.
  • Time. A small team programs, welcomes, projects and runs events. Drawing and maintaining seat maps is never the priority.

What would reserved seating online change?

No study measures, for independent cinemas, the effect of online seat choice on sales. So we make no claim about it. What can be said rests on the figures above:

  • A stated expectation. One in five moviegoers who book does so to choose a seat. At a cinema that does not allow it, that reason to book does not exist.
  • The same service as the multiplex down the street. For moviegoers, choosing a seat has become the norm at chain cinemas.
  • A flow built for the phone. The seat map is read first on a small screen.
  • Less uncertainty for groups. A family or a group of friends knows, before paying, that they will sit together.

What these figures do not say

  • “Non-chain” is not “arthouse.” The two groups overlap without being the same: chains run arthouse screens too. The CNC does not publish these figures for arthouse cinemas.
  • They are statements. Exhibitors estimate their sales; moviegoers describe their last outing.
  • Single-screen cinemas are under-represented in the survey of exhibitors, according to the CNC itself.
  • Seat choice is only measured among cinemas that sell online.
  • The data is French. Other markets may differ.
  • None of these studies measures the effect of a view from the seat. We know of none for cinemas.

Frequently asked questions

Do independent cinemas offer reserved seating online?

Rarely, where it has been measured. In France, among non-chain cinemas that sell tickets online, 5.1% let moviegoers choose their seat, against 95.7% of chain cinemas, according to the CNC’s 2025 survey of exhibitors.

How many independent cinemas sell tickets online?

In France, 53.8% of non-chain cinemas and 37.6% of single-screen cinemas sold tickets online in 2025, against all chains and multiplexes, according to the CNC.

Does a cinema need a seat map to offer reserved seating?

Yes, and a ticketing system that can sell numbered seats. A cinema without a seat map can have one built from photos of its auditoriums, or from measurements taken on site; VisionViewer offers both, along with the 3D view from each seat.

Why do moviegoers want to choose their seat?

In France, 22.4% of moviegoers who book do so to be able to choose their seat, and 28.6% of seniors, according to the CNC’s 2026 study of cinema-going practices.
