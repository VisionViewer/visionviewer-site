Cinema ticketing software: add a 3D seat view instead of building one

You build cinema ticketing software, a point-of-sale system or a booking platform. Your cinema customers may want to show moviegoers the view from each seat. Here is what it takes to build that in-house, and how a seat view API like VisionViewer plugs in instead.

In short

  • A 3D view from the seat is more than a 3D renderer. It takes an accurate model of every auditorium, fast display on phones, and upkeep over time.
  • VisionViewer is added to your software with one tag and one call: the 3D view alone, opened from your seat map, or our 2D seat map with the 3D view.
  • Your seat identifiers stay yours. The mapping is recorded once, auditorium by auditorium.
  • Your ticketing system decides at payment. VisionViewer stores no showtimes, no availability and no sales: only the shape of the auditoriums.
  • A test key works right away on your developer’s machine.

What is a 3D seat view in a ticketing flow?

A 3D seat view is an image of the screen as it looks from one specific seat, shown during booking, before payment. In VisionViewer, it is computed in the browser from the auditorium’s dimensions: the camera sits at the eyes of a seated moviegoer, 1.15 m above the floor of their row, looking at the center of the screen.

For a software vendor, the question is not whether the feature helps your cinemas. It is who builds it, and who keeps it running.

Why should a cinema ticketing vendor care?

Because the seat sits at the heart of buying online. In a study carried out by Vertigo Research for Webedia Movies Pro among more than 100,000 US moviegoers in 2018, 28% of online buyers say they chose their cinema because it offers reserved seating. We go through those numbers, and their limits, in our article on what the studies say.

Yet on the cinema ticketing side, the seat map is 2D. Among the systems we looked at for our comparison of view-from-seat software, only one advertises a 360-degree view from the selected seat, in its mobile app. A seat map tells you where the seat is. It does not tell you what you will see from it.

What does it take to build a seat view in-house?

We do not know your team or its costs, so we will not put a figure on it for you. What we can describe is the work we had to do, which an in-house team would face too.

A model of every auditorium

An accurate view needs the real geometry of the room: the width, height and bottom edge of the screen, its curvature, the distance to the first row, the spacing between rows, the height of each step, the width of the seats and the gap between them, the aisles. Then the layout: wheelchair spaces, double seats, positions with no seat. Your software knows seat identifiers, rarely these dimensions. Someone has to collect them, room by room, at each of your cinemas, and update them when a room is refitted.

3D rendering on phones

The view has to work on moviegoers’ phones, not only on a desktop. The scene must run smoothly there, with the right camera for each seat, and for groups: we take the view from the center of the group. And seats get sold while the moviegoer is looking.

Speed

A view that lags creates doubt at the worst moment, right before payment. Our first version made the seat map usable in 2.8 seconds on slow 4G. We had to change how the page arrives, draw the room before the code loads, and download the 3D view ahead of time while the moviegoer looks at the seat map. Today, on a first visit over slow 4G, on an emulated phone, the seat map is usable in 0.65 seconds and the 3D view shows its first frame in 0.87 seconds. The details are in our article on speed.

Maintenance

Speed you win is speed you lose again, one fix at a time. Every VisionViewer build ends with size and timing thresholds: if they are exceeded, the release is blocked. Add browsers and auditoriums that change. It is a product in its own right, next to yours.

How does the VisionViewer seat view API plug into ticketing software?

A <script> tag loads the SDK, then one call does the rest. There are two integrations, depending on what your software already shows:

Your softwareThe callWhat happens
Already has a seat mapVisionViewer.open()A button on your seat map opens the 3D view full screen, from the selected seats. When the moviegoer confirms, the seats come back to you (onConfirm).
Has no seat mapVisionViewer.mountPlan()Our 2D seat map goes into a block of your page, in a light or dark theme. Your page receives every change of selection (onChange).

Either way, your page speaks in its own seat identifiers. If your software calls G9 “7-9”, the mapping is recorded once, auditorium by auditorium: nothing to translate on each call.

Sold seats come in through occupied: a snapshot taken when the view opens. If a seat sells while the moviegoer is looking, your page sends the new list (update); if that seat was selected, it is removed, and your page is told. In every case, your ticketing system decides at payment.

The full code for both integrations is in our step-by-step integration guide, and every option is in the technical guide.

What data goes through VisionViewer?

  • What we keep: the shape of the auditoriums, and each cinema’s logo, projected on the screen in the 3D view.
  • What we do not keep: showtimes, prices, availability, sales, payments.
  • Moviegoers: no account, no cookie, no storage in their browser.
  • What we count: per day and per auditorium, seat maps displayed, 3D views opened, choices confirmed from the 3D view and, if the confirmation page reports them (reportOrder()), orders.

How can you try it before pitching it to your cinemas?

A test key (vv_test_…) works right away on localhost, on any port. Your developer wires the view to your own seat map, on their machine, before anything goes live. The view then shows a “test version” notice.

The live key (vv_live_…) only works on declared domains, with no notice. A declared domain is an exact name: no wildcards.

A cinema with no seat map or dimensions is not a blocker: we build its auditorium from a few photos, or an engineer measures it on site.

What VisionViewer does not do

  • It is made for cinemas, and only for them. No stadiums, no theaters.
  • The model describes straight rows, on a floor with a regular slope and a single level. Curved rows and balconies are not supported yet.
  • The view is computed, not photographed. It renders the distance, the angle and the height of the screen, not the room’s decoration.
  • We have no cinema results to publish yet. We credit VisionViewer with no sales gain we have not measured.

Frequently asked questions

Can a cinema ticketing vendor add a 3D seat view without replacing its seat map?

Yes. With VisionViewer.open(), a button on your existing seat map opens the 3D view from the selected seats, then hands back the seats the moviegoer settles on. Your seat map does not change.

Does VisionViewer store sales or availability?

No. VisionViewer only keeps the shape of the auditoriums. Sold seats are passed in when the view opens (occupied) and updated as needed (update); your ticketing system decides at payment.

Do we have to rename our seats to use the seat view API?

No. Your page uses your own seat identifiers. The mapping to the modeled auditorium is recorded once, auditorium by auditorium.
