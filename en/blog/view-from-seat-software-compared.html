View-from-seat and interactive seat map software, compared

Several tools show a buyer what they will see from their seat. They do not do it the same way, nor for the same venues. Here are fourteen of them, described from what their vendors publish, and what that means for a cinema.

In short

  • Four ways to show the view: the 2D seat map, the photo taken from the seat, the 360° panorama drawn from a digital model of the venue, and the 3D view computed seat by seat.
  • View-from-seat tools are aimed first at stadiums, arenas and theaters. On the pages we consulted, none of these vendors mentions cinemas.
  • Cinema ticketing systems provide a 2D seat map. Among those we consulted, only one, Compeso, advertises a 360-degree view from the selected seat, in its mobile app.
  • Two seat map vendors publish their prices: Seats.io and Seatmap.pro. The others quote on request.
  • VisionViewer is our product: it computes a 3D view from every seat of a cinema auditorium, and is added to the existing ticketing system.

Who is writing, and how

VisionViewer is our product: this comparison is written by an interested party. To keep it verifiable, we hold to one rule. Every statement about another product restates what its vendor writes on a public page, which we link to. Those pages were consulted on November 19 and 20, 2026.

Four ways to show the view

The 2D seat map

The seat map shows where the seat is: its row, its distance from the aisle, its position relative to the screen. It does not show what you see from it. It is what ticketing systems provide, and it is where everything starts.

The photo taken from the seat

A photo shows the venue as it is, with its light and its materials. One has to be taken per seat, or per section, and taken again when the venue changes.

The 360° panorama of a digital model

The venue is first rebuilt in 3D, from architectural drawings or a laser scan. From it, an image or a panorama is produced for each seat, in which the buyer can look around. This is the approach of large venues: stadiums, arenas, opera houses.

The 3D view computed seat by seat

The auditorium is described by its dimensions: screen, stepped floor, rows, seats. The view is computed in the browser, for the chosen seat, at the moment the moviegoer chooses it. This is VisionViewer’s approach, for cinema auditoriums.

The tools of stadiums, arenas and theaters

3D Digital Venue (Barcelona, part of Mobile Media Content) rebuilds the venue as a true-to-scale digital model, and produces maps and 360° panoramas from the seats. Its assets are integrated into sales channels through its own API, called DVM. Its site mentions stadiums, arenas, theaters and racetracks.

PACIFA 3D offers a realistic 360° view from the selected seat, and an API to integrate into a ticketing platform. Its site addresses sports clubs, culture and live music, and events.

Seat Preevue, by the London company Preevue, provides photorealistic views from every seat of an auditorium. They are rendered from a digital model, built from a laser scan for an existing building or from architectural drawings for a new one. The vendor lists integrations with Tessitura, Spektrix, Seats.io and AudienceView, and an annual license based on the venue’s capacity.

Virtual Venue is Ticketmaster’s 3D tool: the buyer explores 360-degree views from the selected section. It is a feature of Ticketmaster’s ticketing. In June 2025, the company announced its launch in a first arena outside North America, Co-op Live.

TickX (Manchester) presents a first-person 360° view, in which the buyer clicks a seat to explore the venue. Its page does not say whether it relies on photos or on a 3D model.

A View From My Seat starts from the audience: fans share the photos they took from their seats, at more than 6,000 stadiums, arenas and theaters according to its app listing.

Interactive seat maps

Seats.io (Belgium) provides interactive seating charts to ticketing platforms. Its 3D mode is generated from an existing 2D chart, with no 3D modeling, and lets the buyer enter the view from any seat; on a device that cannot handle it, the chart falls back to 2D. Its prices are public: the first plan costs €450 a year for 2,500 used seats, then billing is per used seat.

Seatmap.pro (Cyprus) provides interactive 2D seat maps, integrated through an npm package, an iframe or an API. Rather than a 3D model, each section can carry up to ten photos, such as the view from its seats. Its prices are public, from the free plan to €20,000 a year for an on-premise installation.

And for a cinema?

The tools above do not mention cinemas on the pages we consulted. That does not mean they cannot serve them: only that their vendors talk about other venues.

On the cinema ticketing side, the seat map is 2D. Vista (New Zealand) provides the seat layout and availability through its API; the cinema’s interface draws them. Veezi, from the same group, offers seat selection in its online sales, for independent cinemas. In France, Monnaie Services offers sales on a seat map, enabled showtime by showtime.

Compeso (Germany) is the exception: the page of its mobile app advertises a 360-degree view from the selected seat, to check the view of the screen before buying. It does not say whether that view is a photo, a panorama or a 3D rendering.

Finally, CinemaView is a free simulator for the public: it shows the screen from the seats of reference auditoriums (IMAX, Dolby Cinema, ScreenX, 4DX). Its author states that these are reference rooms, not real venues; we found no integration with a ticketing system.

Where VisionViewer stands

VisionViewer shows every moviegoer the view from their cinema seat, in 3D, before payment. What it does, and what it does not do:

  • It is made for cinemas, and only for them: a screen, a stepped floor, rows. No stadiums, no theaters with balconies.
  • The view is computed, not photographed. It renders the geometry of the auditorium: the distance, the angle, the height of the screen from the seat. It does not render its decoration.
  • Each auditorium is described by its dimensions. Today, the model describes straight rows, on a floor with a regular slope and a single level.
  • It is added to the ticketing system, with one tag and one call. Showtimes, availability and payment stay with the cinema. The details are in our step-by-step integration guide.
  • We have no cinema results to publish yet. We credit VisionViewer with no gain we have not measured.

The questions to ask before choosing

  • Which venue? A 60,000-seat stadium and a 150-seat auditorium do not call for the same tool.
  • Where does the model of the venue come from? Photos, a laser scan, architectural drawings, or a few measurements: cost and lead time depend on it.
  • Who keeps the seat map? Some tools replace the existing seat map, others are added to it.
  • With which ticketing system? A feature of one ticketing system, a ready-made integration, or an API to connect.
  • On a phone, how fast? A view that is slow to appear makes people hesitate at the worst moment. Ask for measurements.
  • At what price, and on what basis? Per seat sold, by venue capacity, per year, or by quote.

Frequently asked questions

Is there view-from-seat software for movie theaters?

Yes. VisionViewer computes a 3D view from every seat of a cinema auditorium, inside the booking flow. The cinema ticketing vendor Compeso also advertises a 360-degree view from the selected seat, in its mobile app. Most other view-from-seat tools are aimed at stadiums, arenas and theaters.

What is the difference between a 360° panorama and a computed 3D view?

A panorama is an image prepared in advance for a seat, from a digital model or a photo. A computed 3D view is produced in the browser at the moment the moviegoer chooses their seat, from the auditorium’s dimensions.

Do you have to replace your seat map to add a view from the seat?

It depends on the tool. Some provide the seat map itself. Others, like VisionViewer, are added to the existing seat map with a button, and only provide one if the cinema has none.

How much does an interactive seat map cost?

Two vendors publish their prices. At Seats.io, the first plan costs €450 a year for 2,500 used seats. At Seatmap.pro, plans range from €0 to €20,000 a year. The other vendors consulted quote on request. Prices recorded on November 20, 2026.
